## 31.1 IMPORT -- Mini DayZ 2's art into the remake's pipeline, and same-named art replaced

The foundation every later Stage 31 track builds on. No gameplay changes here: the art arrives, ready to be used, and where a kind in scope already has art of the same name the remake shows Mini DayZ 2's.

- **Commit the readers.** Move the surveys' decoders into the repo as tools (`tools/mdz2/`: World.bytes's decoder, the APK sprite/prefab/animation readers, from `OriginalData/mdz2/data/_work/` and `decode_world.py`), runnable from the repo with paths to `New Assets` and `packagedUnityAPK` given (read from an environment variable or a gitignored config, as `OriginalData/` is). They write their output under the gitignored `OriginalData/mdz2/` as now. A tool that lacks its inputs says so and exits, never fails a lint (as `c2data.missing` does for 1.0's data).
- **An importer** (`tools/mdz2/import.py`): from a list of what is in scope (a data file you write, e.g. `tools/mdz2/scope.lua` or `.json`, by kind: buildings, unworn items, objects, creatures, UI, ground), cut each wanted sprite out of its `Texture2D` sheet by its APK rect into `Assets/Sprites/<object>/<frame>.png` in the rip's layout, and write each frame's pivot as its anchor where the game reads anchors (`data/sprite_anchors.lua`, by frame key, or a generated anchors file the game reads beside it -- your choice, said). Decide and say how a Mini DayZ 2 object maps onto a remake object: a building's parts (`<b>` front, `<b>_int` floor and back walls) as the remake already names its buildings; an item's `_Icon`, `_Dropped` (and `_BigIcon`) as the remake names item art (`Assets/Icons`, the ground sprites; read `docs/original_data.md` and `game/data/item_art.lua`); a creature's sheet as an animated object with its actions and directions from `animations.json` (`idle`, `run`, ..., the remake's names; Mini DayZ 2 has three directions and its left is the right mirrored: say how the game will draw left); UI as single textures. Then `python tools/build.py --only atlas` and `--only animations` (and `--only icons`), and the generated files that change committed.
- **Replace same-named art in scope**: where `match.csv` says a sheet in scope is identical, nothing changes; where it is changed or redrawn (cars retouched, the gun shop narrower, doorways cut in the fire station and the green garage, recoloured military floors, item icons redrawn), the remake now shows Mini DayZ 2's. Nothing of the player, its clothing or its weapons changes -- not the worn sheets, not the weapon sheets, not their icons. Say every replaced sheet, and whether any replacement moves what the game reads (an anchor, a collider, a size: the gun shop's 207 px against 243) and what you did about it (a building's colliders and anchors are 31.3's to redo from Mini DayZ 2's layout; here keep the remake working).
- **Import everything in scope now, or by kind?** Import what the later tracks need: buildings, unworn items, objects, creatures (the 23 new infected, the 20 bandits, the new animals), UI, ground. If the atlas would grow past what the web build can carry, say what it costs (pages, MB of `game.data`, load time on a phone at 4x CPU with the web build in your scratch dir) and import by kind behind the scope file, so each later track turns its own kind on.

- **Licences and source**: say in `docs/` where this art comes from, as `docs/original_data.md` says of 1.0's.
- Scenarios (lint, tooling): every imported frame's pixels are its APK rect's in its sheet (a sample per kind); the anchors are the pivots; the player's, the clothing's and the weapons' sheets are byte for byte what they were; a replaced sheet is Mini DayZ 2's.
